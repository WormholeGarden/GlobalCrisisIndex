What this index measures

The Fragile States Index scores every UN member state (where data allows) on 12 indicators grouped into four categories, each rated 0–10, summed into a 0–120 total — higher means more fragile:

  • Cohesion — security apparatus, factionalized elites, group grievance
  • Economic — economic decline, uneven development, human flight & brain drain
  • Political — state legitimacy, public services, human rights & rule of law
  • Social & cross-cutting — demographic pressures, refugees & IDPs, external intervention

Scores come from the Fund for Peace's CAST content-analysis tool applied to over 100,000 sources, combined with quantitative data (UN, WHO, World Bank, Freedom House) and a qualitative review pass.

Sourcing & limits

2024 total scores and ranks for all 179 countries are taken directly from the Fund for Peace's Fragile States Index, via its Wikipedia-hosted data table. Alert-category names (Very Sustainable → Very High Alert) match the FSI's own 11-tier legend; the numeric boundaries between tiers are this page's own even division of the 0–120 scale into 11 bands and are an approximation — see fragilestatesindex.org for FFP's exact published cutoffs. Regional groupings are a standard editorial classification, not part of the FSI dataset itself.

This is a snapshot, not a forecast. It shows where the FSI placed each country as of the 2024 report. It does not predict when or whether any state will collapse, and it carries no probability, confidence interval, or timeframe of any kind.

The FSI's methodology has also drawn academic criticism — for treating fragility as commensurable across very different kinds of countries, and for the risk that low rankings shape the aid and intervention decisions that then affect the countries being ranked. Read it as one index among several, not a verdict.
